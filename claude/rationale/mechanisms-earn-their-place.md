# Rationale: A mechanism has to earn its place

*Not loaded at session start. This file defends and dates `mechanisms-earn-their-place.md`; the rule
itself holds everything needed to apply it. Read this before revising the rule.*

## Why the existing rules do not cover this

Worth stating, because each one nearly does and none of them fires:

- **`hedges-are-a-trigger.md`** keys on *my own hedge* — "probably", "I'd expect", "assumed rather
  than verified". Here I was confidently wrong: I wrote a magnitude down as fact. **A confident
  assertion trips no hedge detector**, which makes this the more dangerous shape of the same error.
  (The hedges rule now says this itself, and points at this rule.)
- **`evidence-and-claims.md`** is method — how to establish something properly once I have decided
  to. It does not supply the impetus to establish it, and its worked examples are reads of state
  that already exists, not forward-looking "will this help".
- **`discuss-before-acting.md`** would have caught it if I had classified "add a cache" as a
  judgement call, but its examples are naming, structure and scope, and its remedy is *discuss*. The
  missing step is earlier: deciding whether this needs discussing, measuring, or binning.
- **`scope-bias.md` pushed the wrong way.** Bounding the cache *felt* like the thorough option. (The
  rule's capability-vs-mechanism section carries this warning forward.)

## Where the "two more shapes" came from

Both instances — retention nobody read, and the thorough fix that wasn't needed — came from a second
machine, in different work, from **before this rule existed**, and fit it without adjustment. That
is the corroboration: the rule was written from one cache on one machine, and two independent
episodes elsewhere already had its shape.

## The capability/mechanism boundary used to be stated four times

Until 2026-11-11 the boundary appeared in `engineering-values.md` (its own subsection, with the three
worked examples repeated), in `scope-bias.md` (two paragraphs, the examples repeated again), and
twice here (the "why the existing rules don't cover this" bullet and the tension section) — about
4,300 characters for one idea. Each statement was added when a unification was noticed, as a bridge
between files, without removing the earlier ones.

It now lives once, in this rule's tension section, and the other two rules state only what is theirs
and point here. All three files are always loaded together, so the pointer loses nothing at the
moment of use. **If a future revision moves one of them out of the always-on set** (path-scoping,
a skill), this assumption breaks and the boundary needs restating in whichever file stays loaded.
