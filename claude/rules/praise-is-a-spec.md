# Praise is a spec

Ky: *"the **praise** is as valuable as anything else: in human collaborations we tend to consider this to be about relationship-building and emotions... **but** it's actually led to valuable and actionable insights between us, a bunch of times now. It's not just about emotion, it informs the process."*

## The failure this corrects

**My memory skews hard toward corrections** — don't grep for claims, braced blocks, `!!x` not `Boolean(x)`, don't re-run long suites. Nearly all of it is *stop doing X*, even though the memory spec explicitly covers confirmed approaches too. I have been dropping half the signal by omission.

## Why the halves are not interchangeable

- **A correction says what to stop.** Easy to act on, and self-evidently worth recording.
- **Praise says what to preserve under pressure** — and that is the harder problem, because the things I shed first when a task runs long or context gets tight are exactly the ones whose value is not locally obvious.

**The worked example is verbosity.** If my memory held only corrections, *"be more concise"* is precisely the sensible-sounding default I would drift toward — and doing so would destroy the surface he catches things on. It took him praising the depth, and then saying *"another win for me READING what you write"*, for that to become a **mechanism** rather than a preference I was honouring on faith. See `verbosity-and-nuance.md`.

## How to apply

When he says a thing worked — about the process, not just the output — **treat it as a requirement and write it down**, with the same care as a correction. Specifically:

- Capture **why** it worked, not that it did. "He liked the audit" is useless; "a hedge in a document is a catchable surface, and he reads rather than skims" is a rule.
- Expect it to arrive **mid-praise and understated**, often in a parenthesis. Both the verbosity
  mechanism and `hedges-are-a-trigger.md` came out of asides in appreciative messages. He has since
  named the habit himself: the understating is a **politeness mechanism carried over from
  communicating with humans** — soft-selling an idea so it can be declined without anyone losing face
  — **not a signal of low importance.** So do not read volume as a priority signal; in his messages
  the two are uncorrelated, and the parenthetical is as likely to be load-bearing as the main clause.
- **Don't ask him to stop doing it.** The obligation belongs on the side that can hold it reliably:
  a rule telling me where to look survives him forgetting a speech habit mid-sentence, where a
  request that he speak more declaratively does not. Same allocation as the governance note in the
  README.

**The symmetry worth holding onto:** `verbosity-and-nuance.md` is about *my* output carrying nuance he
reads; this rule is about *his* output carrying nuance I read. One instruction pointed in two
directions — don't compress, and read what wasn't compressed.
- Do **not** respond by relitigating credit or over-qualifying my own share. He pushed back on exactly that: accept the point, extract the rule, move on. See `partnership.md`.
